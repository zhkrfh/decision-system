# First international post (short-form, Telegram/Bluesky/Mastodon)

I evaluated a job offer 6,000 km from home the way an investor evaluates a bet.

Five scores, each 1–10: p (probability of reaching my target in 5 years), G (upside I can walk away with), C (downside cost, *including opportunity cost*), plus two structural flags — reversibility (R) and compounding (L).

My gut said: p = 6.8. Then I applied one mechanical rule — **divide p by 1.2, a flat 20% optimism tax on self-confidence.**

Expected value dropped from 2.91 to **1.12** (scale −10..10; above 2 means "go all in").

1.12 means: take it, but don't bet your life on it. The two structural flags were high, hinting at unpriced upside — so I set a review date instead of raising the stake blindly.

The method doesn't tell you what to feel. It turns "I can definitely do this" into a piece of paper that says 0.567.
